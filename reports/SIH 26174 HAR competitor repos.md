# ACTUS Out-Reasons a Crowded, Commodity Field

About **60 distinct teams** (roughly 75 public repos, plus about 15 copies) have published work on GitHub for ISRO's SIH 2026 problem statement **SIH26174**, "AI Human Activity Recognition for On-board BAS Experiments". Almost all of them build the same pipeline: a webcam feeds YOLOv8n/YOLO11n and MediaPipe, a hand-to-object distance rule decides interactions, a hand-written JSON/YAML state machine (FSM) tracks the steps, and `pyttsx3` speaks the alerts. Logs go to JSONL, video is "streamed" as MJPEG over HTTP, and the GUI is React or PySide. ACTUS's own pipeline has the same shape, so that half of what we built earns no credit on its own ([Orion README](https://github.com/amitraghvan/Orion/blob/HEAD/README.md); [ACTUS architecture](../stage-3-architecture.md)). Where ACTUS pulls ahead is the reasoning layer. No competitor we inspected has anything like its two-channel belief split, which keeps the protocol prior from hiding a skipped step, or its abstain-and-ask path. None runs 4-rotation pose selection on a real astronaut clip, and none uses a pretrained microgravity action model (MicroG-4M) as a frozen evidence stream. ACTUS falls behind in three places the problem statement names directly. First, about **15 teams ship custom-trained weights**, mostly YOLO detectors trained on their own webcam datasets, while ACTUS ships stock COCO YOLO with no step classifier. Second, every serious rival already streams and records video, and ACTUS's stream-to-IP is **0% built** and marked as cuttable. Third, the ACTUS demo runs offline in batch, while rivals run live at 16–21 FPS. The competition is also less formidable than the READMEs make it look. The most-starred repo runs on mock computer vision. At least two teams publish fabricated metrics or fake weight files. Several "offline" systems call Gemini or Groq in the cloud. The one team that reports an honest held-out number for a skeleton classifier gets **24.78%**. The credibility bar is low, so an honest, reproducible system stands out. Two operational points need attention: **our own repo `BerpyDerpy/Actus` is public and shows up in the same searches competitors run**, and "SIH16174" and "SIH25174" are not IDs for this problem.

## Sixty teams, one template: the field has already converged

The problem statement exists only in SIH 2026, as **SIH26174** (serial no. 174, ISRO, Software, Space Technology) ([SIH 2026 PS list mirror](https://github.com/vedantchalke36/sih-2026-problem-statements/tree/main/ps_2026)). On 27 September the official portal showed **52 of 500 idea slots taken**, with submissions closing **30 September 2026** ([sih.gov.in](https://sih.gov.in/sih2026PS)). The Grand Finale is a 36-hour hackathon in December ([Reskilll](https://reskilll.com/blogs/smart-india-hackathon-2026-launched-timeline-registration-how-to-participate/)). The public repos were all created between 23 August and 27 September 2026, and none has more than 5 stars. Star counts therefore say nothing about quality, and reading the code is the only way to judge a team. The most-starred and most-forked repo, HOMOSENSYS (5★/6 forks), runs on `MockDetector`/`MockPoseEstimator` over a zero array ([monitoring_service.py](https://github.com/rudra00434/HOMOSENSYS-AI-Assistant/blob/HEAD/backend/services/monitoring_service.py)).

Counting keyword and dependency matches across roughly 75 cloned repos shows how uniform the field is. MediaPipe appears in about **45 repos**, YOLO-pose in about 10, and RTMPose in only one (KRAMA, and there only in config) ([KRAMA README](https://github.com/Nimbus3001/KRAMA/blob/HEAD/KRAMA/README.md)). `pyttsx3` appears in about 35, browser Web Speech in about 12 and Piper in about 5. In almost every repo, "stream to a specific IP" means a Flask or FastAPI MJPEG endpoint. RTSP shows up in about 10, mostly as an input source or a README claim, and only BASGUARD actually pushes RTSP, via GStreamer/MediaMTX ([BASGUARD README](https://github.com/Shre-Sat/BASGUARD/blob/HEAD/README.md)). Rule-based sequencing dominates. The few trained temporal models are one ST-GCN (Orion), an XGBoost (Hangla Coders), several RandomForests, about five LSTM/GRU variants, a TCN and a zero-shot X-CLIP ([Kartikey2006 xclip_har.py](https://github.com/Kartikey2006/Astra/blob/HEAD/backend/app/har/xclip_har.py)). All of these components are available off the shelf, from Ultralytics (62K stars, including a ready-made "AI Gym" joint-angle pattern), MediaPipe (37K stars) and the widely cloned keypoints-to-LSTM tutorial ([Ultralytics Solutions](https://docs.ultralytics.com/solutions/); [ActionDetectionforSignLanguage](https://github.com/nicknochnack/ActionDetectionforSignLanguage)). The research repos on procedural mistake detection (EgoPER, Assembly101, CaptainCook4D, PREGO) all assume pre-extracted features on egocentric Earth footage. None offers a "webcam in, alerts out" system a team could fork ([EgoPER](https://github.com/robert80203/EgoPER_official); [review arXiv 2510.19292](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19292)).

Outside GitHub, material is thin. There are **10 YouTube idea-submission demos** from at least 9 teams. We found nothing for this problem on Medium, Devfolio, Unstop, Hugging Face, Kaggle or Roboflow Universe ([KRIYA video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_7xYEvzF8U4); [HF datasets API](https://huggingface.co/api/datasets/jialeh23/red-cube-yellow-box-v2)). The videos show the same template, with three additions worth noting. Several teams demo **more than one protocol**: ORION (Team WayFinders) shows three, Deadcoding two and KRIYA two ([ORION video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ih-ViXyOVpQ); [Deadcoding video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PzQ65QNYl0A)). ORION adds an offline voice assistant that answers "what should I do next?". And **KRIYA runs its second version on real microgravity footage** of an 8-step crew-nutrition protocol and flags a skipped step ([KRIYA video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_7xYEvzF8U4)). ACTUS is therefore not the only team with a real-orbit clip. Some GitHub and video entries appear to belong to the same team, though these links are inferences. The TARA video's member list includes Harsh Audichya and Krishna Kankane, which matches the [T-A-R-A](https://github.com/HarshAudichya/T-A-R-A-for-mac-with-audio-alert) and [isro-bas-har](https://github.com/krishnakankane/isro-bas-har) repos ([TARA video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3e-GRiH3uxA)). Pranvkumar and Krishna105-ui both describe the same "AEGIS" system ([Krishna105 README](https://github.com/Krishna105-ui/Astronaut-Activity-Recognition-System/blob/HEAD/README.md)). The ORION video, on the other hand, appears zone- and marker-based, while `amitraghvan/Orion` is pose- and ST-GCN-based, so the two may not be the same team.

## Seven rivals worth watching, and what each does better

Most novel ideas in the field fall into a handful of clusters. Two teams run real 3D human mesh recovery (HMR), both offline only. Some use local vision-language models (VLMs) to judge whether a step happened. Two answer the "relative to the payload rack" requirement with fiducial markers. A few stand out for evaluation rigour. Others add assurance features (signed logs, fault injection, biometric gates), use different sequence formalisms (Petri nets, entropy gating, HMM smoothing, a Rust FSM), or use open-vocabulary detection. The teams below come closest to ACTUS, either in technical depth or in overlap with our distinctive claims.

Where ACTUS is genuinely ahead is the decision layer. The field's sequencing is almost entirely deterministic FSMs driven by per-frame thresholds. The closest relatives of ACTUS's design are Orion's entropy gate and STEP_UNCERTAIN state, Rahulrwt99's rule of advancing only after repeated observations above threshold, and notaditya28's two-reader cross-check ([decision_engine.py](https://github.com/amitraghvan/Orion/blob/HEAD/app/intelligence/decision_engine.py); [Rahulrwt99 README](https://github.com/Rahulrwt99/bas-experiment-assistant/blob/HEAD/README.md)). None of them separates prior-free evidence from a prior-informed posterior, and none turns uncertainty into a spoken yes/no question to the astronaut. The orientation work is also different in kind. No repo we surveyed advertises rotation test-time augmentation with confidence selection, or keeps a pre-canonical torso vector as a separate rack-relative channel ([ACTUS architecture §2](../stage-3-architecture.md)). Both ideas are cheap to copy once a judge sees them, so they work as a demo moment, not a moat. The strongest claim, MicroG-4M as a frozen evidence stream, is uncontested, but it is also fragile. The repo has 8 stars, depends on PySlowFast, and its BENCHMARK.md returned 404, so checkpoint availability is still unverified ([MicroG-4M repo](https://github.com/lei-qi-233/MicroG-4M)). If the Kinetics substitute ships instead, that stream becomes commodity. The decision layer and the rotation demo would then carry the whole differentiation story.

Where the field is stronger is less flattering, and some of it is by design. Mapping ACTUS's features against open-source prior art puts about 60–70% of its visible surface in the commodity column: detector, pose, FSM, voice, log, GUI and stream. Orion already shows a well-built version of our spine with a trained skeleton model ([Orion README](https://github.com/amitraghvan/Orion/blob/HEAD/README.md)). The problem statement asks for "dataset generation to train model for object detection, pose estimation and hand-object interaction" and for "a trained AI model", and many rivals can point to a fine-tuned detector with mAP numbers while ACTUS points to stock COCO. Orion's result partly vindicates our choice. Its ST-GCN falls from 95.56% to **24.78%** on held-out subjects after training on 20 videos, which supports rule-based logic over pretrained evidence rather than a thinly trained classifier. But judges who read the deliverable list literally will still see a gap. ACTUS's use of a bottle procedure is also a mild exposure: arnab9957, BASGUARD, Orion, Pranvkumar and Telemetrix all implement ISRO's own red/yellow box sample, which gives judges a like-for-like comparison ACTUS cannot offer ([Telemetrix video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z2HCzZ9d1JY)). Finally, "real orbit footage" alone no longer sets ACTUS apart, because KRIYA and mihikahetamsaria both show it. ACTUS's framing therefore has to rest on the skeleton as the Earth-to-orbit bridge, the rotation test, and MicroG-4M evidence, not on the clip.

## Many headline numbers collapse under inspection

The claims differ sharply in how well they hold up. Two teams publish outright fabrications. Monishwarann/SIH headlines **94.61% accuracy**, but its `evaluate.py` sets each class accuracy to `round(0.91 + np.random.uniform(0.0, 0.07), 4)`, and its `activity_model.pt` and `.onnx` are 34- and 31-byte text files ([evaluate.py](https://github.com/Monishwarann/SIH/blob/HEAD/training/evaluate.py)). subish345/Astra claims **mAP@0.5 92.4%**, backed by a `.pt` "checkpoint" that is actually a ~1.2 KB JSON file of metrics ([README](https://github.com/subish345/Astra/blob/HEAD/README.md)). hamsadevang849-sys hard-codes `device = "Rad-Hardened Edge TPU"` and `latency_ms = 11.4`, then simulates events ([edge_inference.py](https://github.com/hamsadevang849-sys/AI-Human-Activity-Recognition-for-On-board-BAS-Experiments/blob/HEAD/backend/edge_inference.py)). krishnakankane's metrics JSON reports accuracy and macro-F1 of **1.0**, which points to synthetic or overfit data ([README](https://github.com/krishnakankane/isro-bas-har/blob/HEAD/README.md)). ATLAS streams a "HOI Pearson's r" field whose live computation we could not locate, while a scratch engine hard-codes 0.85/0.15/0.99 ([README](https://github.com/atulharshvardhan2006/ATLAS-FULL-STACK-SIH-26174/blob/HEAD/README.md)).

A second group overstates features rather than numbers. BASGUARD's description promises an "orientation-agnostic 3D human mesh", but no HMR code exists ([BASGUARD](https://github.com/Shre-Sat/BASGUARD)). ORBITA's "HMR (24 SMPL joints)" quietly falls back to a deterministic kinematic lifter flagged `is_fallback=True` ([hmr_pipeline.py](https://github.com/LakshanyaaJ/ORBITA/blob/HEAD/core_ai/perception/hmr_pipeline.py)). Sheen11 and aaryan2203 ship a `StubHMRProvider` ([hmr_interface.py](https://github.com/Sheen11-ing/AI-based-Human-Activity-Recognition-BAS-/blob/HEAD/BAS/src/hmr_interface.py)). arnab9957's "RTSP", "3D generator" and "IMU" turn out to be MJPEG, 2D OpenCV drawing and vision-derived signals. Its 80+ FPS figure is unverified ([video_pipeline.py](https://github.com/arnab9957/realtime-work-detection/blob/HEAD/src/streaming/video_pipeline.py)). Several "fully offline" systems call cloud models: Kartikey2006 calls Gemini ([gemini_detector.py](https://github.com/Kartikey2006/Astra/blob/HEAD/backend/app/vision/gemini_detector.py)), antas06 calls Groq's `gpt-oss-20b` ([parser.py](https://github.com/antas06/exp-ass-bas/blob/HEAD/src/parser.py)), hawk-bas calls Gemini with an Ollama fallback ([gemini_provider.py](https://github.com/k-a-v-i-n-0-0-2/hawk-bas/blob/HEAD/llm/gemini/gemini_provider.py)), and mihikahetamsaria's astronaut path also uses Gemini. About eight READMEs claim Jetson deployment, and no Jetson build scripts were verified. Outside GitHub, a LinkedIn search snippet says Team Innovators (JECRC) placed "Top 5 at SIH 2026 for SIH26174". Fetching the profile did not show that text, and national screening results only come out in October, so the claim can only refer to an internal college hackathon ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/arpitjecrc/); [TechPathDaily](https://techpathdaily.com/sih-2026-official-timeline-is-here/)).

Some numbers stand in the middle: plausible, but not reproduced. These are na124441's 91.1% LSTM test accuracy, which sits next to a separate 57.5% validation figure; Pranvkumar's 84.7%, which may be illustrative; Hangla Coders' 71–76%; and DRISHTI's 97.25%, which is for posture rather than experiment steps. Only Orion (24.78% held-out) and ishwaryajuttu ("**No accuracy figures**") are candid in public ([ishwaryajuttu README](https://github.com/ishwaryajuttu-gif/SIH_2026/blob/HEAD/README.md)). A caveat applies to this whole section: no competitor repo was executed. Every judgement comes from reading source and checking file sizes and headers, which is how the JSON "weights" were caught. Google Drive and Git LFS assets were not inspected.

Two borrowings are not worth it. A local VLM verifier (Qwen3-VL-2B via Ollama) tells a fashionable story, but its latency on edge hardware is unmeasured and it would blur ACTUS's "FSM is the judge, models are sensors" principle. Real HMR is visually impressive, but it carries the SMPL non-commercial licence and heavy compute (WHAM runs at 4.6 fps even on an RTX 3090). ACTUS's cheaper route is defensible as the edge-appropriate choice ([SMPL licence](https://smpl.is.tue.mpg.de/modellicense.html); [FastHMR arXiv 2510.10868](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10868v1)). One more unclaimed opening: **no team has published a dataset or a Hugging Face Space** for this problem, so a small labelled clip set would be a first ([HF API](https://huggingface.co/api/datasets/jialeh23/red-cube-yellow-box-v2)).

## Operational findings that need action before 30 September

**Our repo is visible.** [BerpyDerpy/Actus](https://github.com/BerpyDerpy/Actus) (created 20 Sep, pushed 27 Sep) comes up for the query "astronaut activity recognition in:readme", the same kind of search that surfaced a dozen competitors. The two-channel belief design, the abstain path and the 4-rotation trick are exactly the ideas rivals would find easiest to copy. Unless openness is intended, make the repo private until submission.

**The IDs "SIH16174" and "SIH25174" are wrong for this problem.** The API returns nothing relevant for SIH16174, and "16174" returns only noise. The user saw web-UI hits for SIH16174, but the API could not reproduce them. **25174 was SIH 2025's ISRO fish-catch identification problem** ([ashish-um/fish-sih](https://github.com/ashish-um/fish-sih)). SIH1674 belongs to SIH 2024 projects ([v9pt/SIH1674](https://github.com/v9pt/SIH1674)). Use **SIH26174**, and search for competitors by that ID as well as by "SIH2026174" and "PS-26174", which some teams use ([Hangla-Coders-SIH2026174](https://github.com/MakInShort/Hangla-Coders-SIH2026174)).

**The official sample experiment is truncated at the source**, at "…two smaller boxes of color red and yello". Teams have read it differently: Telemetrix built pink and yellow boxes, and Pranvkumar declined to guess ([sih.gov.in](https://sih.gov.in/sih2026PS); [Telemetrix video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z2HCzZ9d1JY)). The NoBugNinja dump may hold the full text ([NoBugNinja](https://github.com/NoBugNinja/Smart-India-Hackathon-SIH-2026-Problem-Statements)). Competitors encode the sample as "open the outer box, take out red, take out yellow, close".

**Licence corrections to our own documents.** The preliminary research calls 100DOH "non-commercially licensed", but its GitHub code licence is MIT. The terms for its weights and dataset were not checked ([hand_object_detector](https://github.com/ddshan/hand_object_detector)). Piper's active fork is now **GPL-3.0**, not MIT ([piper1-gpl](https://github.com/OHF-Voice/piper1-gpl)). The architecture doc's notes on Ultralytics AGPL-3.0 and VideoMAE-small CC-BY-NC still stand.

**MicroG-4M checkpoints remain unverified.** BENCHMARK.md returned 404, which confirms the demo plan's 3-hour time box and honest-substitute fallback ([MicroG-4M](https://github.com/lei-qi-233/MicroG-4M)). **Submission density is low.** 52 of 500 slots were taken three days before the deadline, while about 60 teams are visible on GitHub, many of them apparently from internal college rounds ([sih.gov.in](https://sih.gov.in/sih2026PS)).

**Coverage limits.** GitHub code search needs authentication and was not run, so repos that mention 26174 only in source code are missed. Private repos, Reddit and team Instagram reels were not reachable.

## Conclusion

This competition will not be won on pipeline breadth, because nearly every serious entrant already has the full detector → pose → FSM → voice → log → stream → GUI chain, and several have it more complete than ACTUS does today. It will be won on **credibility and on the reasoning layer**. On credibility, a field where the most-starred entry runs on mocks and at least two teams generate their metrics with random numbers gives ACTUS's honesty list more weight than usual. On reasoning, the parts no rival has built are the prior-free skip channel, the abstain-and-ask path, and the orientation test demonstrated on rotated orbit footage. The pitch should lead with those, and it should present the lack of a trained step classifier as a deliberate choice, backed by Orion's 95.56% → 24.78% collapse, rather than as an omission.

The risk runs the other way on literal deliverables. A judge ticking boxes will see ACTUS without video streaming, without a custom dataset, without a trained model of its own, and without live operation. Two of those can be closed in hours (a real RTSP push plus local recording, and a red/yellow box YAML), and closing them matters more than any additional modelling. If MicroG-4M fails to load, the differentiation story shrinks to the decision layer and the rotation test. That is still unique in this field, but only if it is shown working rather than described, and only if the public repo stops advertising it to the teams most likely to copy it.
