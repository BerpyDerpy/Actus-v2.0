# Skeleton pipelines win the race for lightweight home HAR

For a laptop GPU with 4-8GB of VRAM feeding an FSM that validates experiment steps, the strongest available option is not a single model but a **two-stage skeleton pipeline**: a fast pose estimator (RTMPose or MediaPipe Pose) feeding a lightweight graph-convolutional classifier (ST-GCN++, or PoseC3D if headroom allows), trained on NTU RGB+D's daily-action subset and fine-tuned on a small home-recorded set. This combination is the only route in the research with confirmed sub-3M-parameter model sizes, confirmed 90-430+ FPS component benchmarks on GPUs at or below the target tier, and the widest set of directly relevant everyday-action classes (eating, drinking, wearing a jacket, reading, sitting/standing, waving, and more, per **NTU RGB+D's 82 "daily action" classes out of 120** ([ROSE Lab dataset page](https://rose1.ntu.edu.sg/dataset/actionRecognition/), [NTU RGB+D 120 paper](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1109/tpami.2019.2916873))). RGB-video-only models (X3D, TSM, MoViNet) are viable alternatives with confirmed edge-device throughput (e.g., **TSM: 74.6-117.6 FPS on Jetson Nano/TX2** ([TSM edge paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2109.13227v1))), but their pretraining corpora (Kinetics) are sports/hobby-heavy rather than home-action-focused, and none has a laptop-GPU-specific benchmark. Hand-object-interaction detectors (100DOH, EgoHOS) exist but are research-grade, non-commercially licensed, and unbenchmarked for speed — useful as inspiration for the FSM's "picked up X" checks but not drop-in real-time components. No source in either research pass reports a combined pose+classification or full end-to-end FPS/VRAM figure measured specifically on an RTX 3050/3060/4060 mobile GPU, so the final pipeline choice will need empirical validation on the target hardware, though every component-level number found strongly implies real-time (30+ FPS) headroom on that hardware class.

## The GCN family gives the best accuracy-per-parameter ratio for skeleton input

The dominant lightweight architecture line for skeleton-based action recognition is the graph convolutional network (GCN) family: ST-GCN (2018) evolved through 2s-AGCN (adaptive/attention-based topology) into **ST-GCN++**, an efficiency-focused re-engineering that cuts compute roughly in half while matching or beating the earlier models' accuracy. Confirmed numbers from the MMAction2 model zoo put ST-GCN++ at **1.4M parameters and 1.95-2.96 GFLOPs per stream**, reaching **89.29% joint-only / 92.77% four-stream-fusion top-1 on NTU RGB+D 60 cross-subject (2D pose input)** ([MMAction2 Skeleton Model Zoo](https://mmaction2.readthedocs.io/en/dev-1.x/model_zoo/skeleton.html)) — versus ST-GCN's 3.1M params/3.8-5.7 GFLOPs and 2s-AGCN's 3.5M params/4.4-6.5 GFLOPs for similar accuracy. This makes ST-GCN++ the safest confirmed choice for a 4-8GB VRAM budget: a single-stream (joint-only) deployment already clears 89% accuracy at sub-3 GFLOPs, and even the four-stream fusion variant stays under 12 GFLOPs total. **PoseC3D** takes a different approach — treating stacked 2D pose heatmaps as a 3D CNN "clip" on a SlowOnly-R50 backbone rather than a graph — and trades some efficiency for **94.0% fused accuracy on NTU60 X-Sub**, plus documented robustness to noisy pose input and the widest cross-dataset validation of any skeleton model found (FineGYM 93.5%, UCF101 86.8%, HMDB51 69.6%, even Kinetics-400 47.4%) ([MMAction2 Skeleton Model Zoo](https://mmaction2.readthedocs.io/en/dev-1.x/model_zoo/skeleton.html)). Newer post-2020 architectures — **CTR-GCN** (channel-wise topology refinement, ICCV 2021) and **HD-GCN** (hierarchically decomposed, ICCV 2023, MIT-licensed) — are widely cited as current state-of-the-art and both ship pretrained NTU60/120 weights, but their exact accuracy/parameter/FLOP figures could not be extracted from the sources reached in this research and should be treated as "commonly cited as SOTA-tier but unverified" pending a follow-up read of the arXiv PDFs directly, since Papers-with-Code's leaderboard was blocked by robots.txt. Feeding these classifiers, the pose-estimation front end has two confirmed real-time winners: **RTMPose** (Apache 2.0, OpenMMLab) reports **90+ FPS on an Intel i7 CPU and 430+ FPS on an NVIDIA GTX 1660 Ti** — a lower-tier card than an RTX 3050/3060/4060 mobile, implying even higher throughput on the target hardware — at 72-76% COCO AP, while **MediaPipe Pose/BlazePose** hits 30+ FPS even on a phone CPU at roughly 78% COCO AP with a permissive Apache 2.0 license ([RTMPose paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.07399v2), [pose-tracking stack overview](https://www.forasoft.com/learn/ai-for-video-engineering/articles-ai/openpose-mediapipe-rtmpose-pose-tracking)). OpenPose, by contrast, needs a high-end desktop GPU to hit just 8-22 FPS and carries a non-commercial license, ruling it out. Because the classifier operates on a short temporal window (e.g., every 30-64 frames) rather than every frame, and both pose estimators leave large compute headroom on a laptop GPU, an end-to-end pose+classification pipeline at 24-30 FPS is very likely feasible — though this remains an inference from component-level numbers rather than a directly cited combined benchmark, and is the single largest evidence gap in this research.

## RGB-video models trade dataset fit for architectural maturity

If skeleton extraction proves unreliable for a given action (e.g., partial occlusion during hand-object manipulation), RGB-video-based classifiers are the fallback, and three families have genuinely lightweight, real-time-proven variants. **TSM (Temporal Shift Module)**, a ResNet-50-based 2D CNN with zero-cost temporal shifting, is the best-benchmarked of the group on constrained hardware: **74.6 FPS on a Jetson Nano at 4.5W, 117.6 FPS on a Jetson TX2**, and 74.1-76.3% top-1 on Kinetics-400 ([TSM edge paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2109.13227v1)) — since a laptop RTX 3050/3060/4060 sits well above Jetson-class compute, TSM should clear real-time throughput with room to spare. **MoViNet**, Google's mobile-optimized 3D CNN family, offers the best documented accuracy/memory Pareto frontier: MoViNet-A0 (3.1M params, 2.71 GFLOPs) hits 71.5% top-1 Kinetics-600 at 3.7ms/frame on CPU, while its streaming variants cut peak memory by up to 92% (A5-Stream: 2040MB to 171MB) for roughly 1% accuracy loss, reaching 82.0% top-1 in continuous/causal inference mode ([MoViNets paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.11511v1)) — a meaningful advantage if the FSM needs always-on inference rather than clip-by-clip batches, though no confirmed PyTorch implementation with pretrained weights was found (the original release is TensorFlow-based). **X3D** rounds out the group with 73-76% top-1 Kinetics-400 accuracy at a few GFLOPs (X3D-S/M, 3.79-6.15M params) and first-party pretrained checkpoints via PyTorchVideo and MMAction2, making it the most turnkey default despite MMAction2's fetched README noting X3D fine-tuning support was still "coming soon" at the time captured. Heavier options — Video Swin-Tiny (28.2M params, 88 GFLOPs, 78.9% top-1) and UniFormer-XS (34.2 GFLOPs, 78.6% top-1, MIT-licensed) — offer 78-79% accuracy at a real but plausible compute cost for an 8GB card, though no direct consumer-GPU FPS numbers exist for either. VideoMAE-small reaches the highest accuracy of the small models (79.0% top-1) but is CC-BY-NC-4.0 licensed, a real constraint if the project has any competition or commercial redistribution angle — a consideration flagged given the user's rocketry/CanSat competition context, where licensing terms have mattered before. Critically, none of these RGB families has a benchmark on a laptop-mobile RTX GPU specifically; all cited figures come from Jetson Nano/TX2, Raspberry Pi, phone GPUs, or datacenter cards (V100, P100, T4), so the team should treat these as directional evidence, not guaranteed laptop performance. A 2026 edge-deployment paper (X3D-UGT, arXiv 2602.10818) reports a sub-1M-parameter RGB model reaching 95.10% on NTU60 X-Sub, but it's benchmarked on Jetson Orin Nano, not Kinetics/UCF/HMDB, and it wasn't confirmed to be strictly RGB-only versus using auxiliary pose signals internally — a flag worth resolving before relying on it.

## No turnkey toolkit exists, but MMAction2 and OpenVINO come closest

There is no single "install and it just works" real-time HAR toolkit for arbitrary everyday home actions; the practical path is composing a pipeline from a small number of maintained ecosystems. **MMAction2** (OpenMMLab) is the most complete research toolkit, hosting nearly every architecture discussed above — ST-GCN/ST-GCN++/AAGCN, PoseC3D, X3D, TSM, Video Swin, UniFormer, SlowFast — under one consistent fine-tuning framework, paired with its companion deployment tool **MMDeploy** for ONNX/TensorRT/OpenVINO export ([MMDeploy MMAction2 docs](https://mmdeploy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/04-supported-codebases/mmaction2.html)). This makes it the single best entry point for a small team wanting to avoid mixing multiple frameworks. **OpenVINO** ships an official, purpose-built real-time Action Recognition demo — a Kinetics-400-trained encoder-decoder pipeline with async/pipelined inference, live FPS display, and CPU as the default target device ([OpenVINO Action Recognition demo](https://github.com/openvinotoolkit/open_model_zoo/blob/master/demos/action_recognition_demo/python/README.md)) — the most genuinely "turnkey" real-time demo found, though its Kinetics-400 classes are coarse and generic and would need replacement or fine-tuning for procedure-specific steps. **Ultralytics YOLO11/YOLO26-pose** exports cleanly to ONNX/TensorRT/OpenVINO and ships a reusable "AI Gym" pattern that counts reps via joint-angle thresholds between keypoints — directly adaptable for rule-based detection of simple actions like sitting/standing/reaching, though it's dual-licensed (AGPL-3.0 or paid commercial) and has no published FPS numbers on laptop-class GPUs specifically, only CPU-ONNX versus datacenter-T4-TensorRT comparisons. For hand-object interaction specifically, **100DOH** (Faster R-CNN hand+state detector, non-commercial license) and **EgoHOS** (Swin-L-backbone segmentation, unspecified license, no FPS numbers) are the two relevant research releases, but neither ships an edge-ready export, and EgoHOS's heavyweight transformer backbone is unlikely to hit real-time on a 4-8GB card without distillation work — 100DOH's simpler two-stage detector is the more promising starting point for re-export to ONNX/TensorRT. Across all three toolkits, the consistent optimization path for an NVIDIA laptop GPU is **ONNX export followed by TensorRT conversion** (via ONNX Runtime's TensorRT execution provider or a toolkit's native export), which every major pipeline (Ultralytics, MMDeploy) already points users toward; OpenVINO is the parallel path if CPU-only deployment becomes necessary. A genuine gap: no published case study benchmarks a full detector+pose+classifier pipeline end-to-end on any RTX 30/40-series mobile GPU, so the team should expect to run their own benchmark pass once a pipeline is assembled — likely the highest-value next step before committing to a specific architecture combination.

## Dataset choice should follow the FSM's granularity, not just size

Two clusters of datasets exist: large general-purpose corpora (Kinetics-400/600/700, UCF101, HMDB51) mixing sports and daily actions, used mainly for backbone pretraining, and purpose-built indoor/daily-living datasets that are far closer to a home-recordable action set. **NTU RGB+D 60/120** is the dominant benchmark for essentially every skeleton model in this catalog, offers 3D skeletons plus RGB/depth/infrared from Kinect v2 cameras, and its 120-class version devotes **82 of 120 classes to daily actions** — eating, drinking, wearing a jacket, reading, phone use, sitting/standing, waving — directly matching the reader's target action list, though it requires an academic Release Agreement and a roughly 1.3-2.3TB download ([ROSE Lab dataset page](https://rose1.ntu.edu.sg/dataset/actionRecognition/)). **PKU-MMD** (Apache-2.0 code license) is a close second for daily-action fit: 41 of its 51 classes are explicitly daily actions (drinking, waving, putting on glasses) captured with the same five-modality Kinect setup as NTU, across 1,076 untrimmed sequences ([PKU-MMD GitHub](https://github.com/ECHO960/PKU-MMD)). For the FSM's specific need to validate *hand-object interaction steps* rather than whole-body gestures, **Something-Something V2** (with its Something-Else bounding-box extension) and **EPIC-KITCHENS-100** (with its VISOR pixel-level hand/object segmentation, 66.6M masks) are the most semantically aligned public resources — both were purpose-built around fine-grained object manipulation rather than scene or gesture recognition ([Something-Else paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/papers/Materzynska_Something-Else_Compositional_Action_Recognition_With_Spatial-Temporal_Interaction_Networks_CVPR_2020_paper.pdf), [VISOR dataset page](https://epic-kitchens.github.io/VISOR/)). **Toyota Smarthome** and **ETRI-Activity3D** are the most explicitly "ADL/elderly-monitoring" datasets (31-51 and 55 daily-activity classes respectively, real apartment or lab settings), but neither had a confirmed model accuracy figure retrievable in this research, and both require institutional data-access requests. For pretrained backbones specifically usable as fine-tuning starting points: the **MMAction2 model zoo** is the single richest source, with ST-GCN/ST-GCN++/AAGCN and PoseC3D checkpoints trained on NTU60/120 and FineGYM, and PoseC3D notably spanning the widest range of datasets (NTU, FineGYM, UCF101, HMDB51, even Kinetics-400) — making it the strongest general-purpose transfer-learning candidate since it only needs 2D pose keypoints (extractable via MediaPipe or RTMPose from ordinary RGB video) rather than Kinect depth data, which matters for a team recording with a single home camera rather than a Kinect array. Toyota Smarthome's own authors additionally distribute pre-extracted I3D (RGB) and AGCN (pose) features as an official transfer-learning starting point ([Toyota Smarthome project page](https://project.inria.fr/toyotasmarthome/)). No 2024-2026 small-scale, reproducibility-focused, RGB-camera-based HAR dataset was found — recent "lightweight" HAR datasets in this window are wearable-sensor (IMU/accelerometer) based, not vision-based — reinforcing that a custom home-recorded dataset, fine-tuned from NTU/PKU-MMD-pretrained weights, is the realistic path rather than adopting an off-the-shelf small dataset.

## Conclusion

The clearest architectural decision this research supports is to build the FSM's perception layer around **pose-estimation-plus-GCN**, not raw-RGB classification: it is the only route with a confirmed sub-3M-parameter, sub-3-GFLOP classifier (ST-GCN++) paired with a confirmed 90-430+ FPS pose front end (RTMPose), backed by a dataset (NTU RGB+D 120's daily-action subset) whose class list overlaps almost one-to-one with the target actions. PoseC3D is worth keeping as a fallback for actions where 2D keypoints prove unreliable, since its heatmap-based representation is more robust to noisy pose input at a modest compute premium. The one recommendation that should shape near-term engineering effort is to close the biggest gap found across both research passes: nobody has published an end-to-end (pose-extraction plus classification) FPS or VRAM benchmark on an RTX 3050/3060/4060 mobile GPU for any of these architectures, so the team's first practical step should be a quick empirical throughput test of RTMPose plus ST-GCN++ on their actual laptop before investing in dataset collection or fine-tuning — the component-level evidence strongly suggests it will clear real-time comfortably, but that remains an inference, not a measurement.
